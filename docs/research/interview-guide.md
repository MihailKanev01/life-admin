# User Interview Guide — Validation

## Goal

Validate whether people have a recurring personal life-admin problem and how they solve it today.

## Interview rules

- Ask about recent real events, not hypothetical preferences.
- Do not pitch the product before the behavioral questions are finished.
- Ask "why?" and "what happened next?".
- Capture exact words users use to describe the problem.
- Record workarounds, not only complaints.

## Opening

"I am researching how people keep track of personal admin such as bills, deadlines, documents, maintenance and things they are waiting on. I am not selling anything. I want to understand how you handle it today."

## Core questions

1. Think about the last time you nearly forgot an important deadline. What happened?
2. What kinds of dates or things do you regularly need to remember?
3. Where do you store that information today?
4. How many different apps/tools are involved?
5. What is annoying about the current setup?
6. When you get a reminder at a bad time, what do you usually do?
7. What information do you often need to search for later?
8. What documents do you keep and where?
9. Which bills or subscriptions do you actively track?
10. Are there things you are currently waiting for from another person or company?
11. Which recurring tasks do you remember manually?
12. Have you ever built a spreadsheet, Notes system, Notion page or similar to solve this? What happened?
13. What made you stop using systems that you tried?
14. When you open a personal organization tool, what do you expect to see first?

## Follow-up prompts

- "Can you show me how you do that today?"
- "When did that last happen?"
- "What did you do then?"
- "Why did you choose that tool?"
- "What happens if you don't do it?"
- "What would you have to remember yourself?"

## Do not ask early

- "Would you use our app?"
- "Would you pay €X?"
- "Do you like this idea?"
- "Would AI help?"

These questions bias the interview toward the solution.

## Synthesis method

After every 3–5 interviews, cluster findings into workflows rather than individual comments.

Examples:
- Renewal/deadline management
- Document retrieval
- Household maintenance
- Recurring payments
- Waiting/follow-up
- Capture/input friction
- Notification fatigue

Do not turn one person's unusual request into an MVP feature without repeated evidence.
