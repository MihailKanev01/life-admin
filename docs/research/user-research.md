# User Research Synthesis

## What people appear to struggle with

Across public user discussions and current life-admin products, recurring themes are:
- too many separate tools;
- remembering deadlines at the right time;
- long overdue lists that become noise;
- difficulty finding information later;
- repetitive household administration;
- uncertainty about what should be tracked in the first place;
- reminders arriving at bad times;
- frustration with productivity apps that add too many features.

## Strong qualitative signals

A June 2026 Reddit discussion emphasized that the product people stick with is often the one with fast input and well-timed reminders rather than the most powerful feature set.

A February 2026 discussion asked specifically for productivity software without feature bloat and described unwanted gamification and unnecessary functionality.

Public life-admin discussions also contain people describing combinations of Notes, Calendar, reminders and spreadsheets as their current system.

## What we should validate in interviews

Do not ask whether people "would use our app". Ask about actual recent behavior.

### Interview prompts

1. Tell me about the last important deadline you nearly forgot.
2. Where do you currently keep bills and renewal dates?
3. Where do you keep warranty/insurance/vehicle documents?
4. What do you do when a reminder appears at a bad time?
5. Which apps do you use for personal admin in a normal week?
6. What information do you repeatedly search for?
7. What are you currently waiting on from another person/company?
8. What recurring household/vehicle tasks do you manually remember?
9. What makes an organization tool annoying enough that you stop using it?
10. What is the first thing you would want to see when opening a personal admin app?

## Interview scoring

For each pain point record:
- frequency
- severity
- current workaround
- time cost
- emotional cost
- willingness to change behavior
- evidence of an existing budget/spend

## Validation target

Aim for 10–15 interviews across at least three user profiles before locking the MVP.

The goal is not statistical significance. The goal is to discover repeated workflows, vocabulary and failure points before implementation.
