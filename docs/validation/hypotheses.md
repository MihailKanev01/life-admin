# Validation Hypotheses

Each hypothesis must be tested before it becomes a product commitment.

## H1 — People experience fragmented personal admin

**Hypothesis:** Target users regularly use multiple tools to manage personal deadlines, documents, bills and reminders.

**Evidence to seek:**
- number and types of tools used;
- manual copying between tools;
- missed or duplicated information;
- stated frustration with maintenance.

**Pass signal:** at least 7 of the first 10 interviews describe a fragmented workflow with meaningful friction.

---

## H2 — The core problem is attention, not storage

**Hypothesis:** Users care more about knowing what needs attention than having a larger repository of information.

**Evidence to seek:**
- what they check first today;
- how they prioritize upcoming items;
- examples of information they own but rarely need;
- whether a simple "what needs attention" view feels valuable.

**Pass signal:** repeated preference for a prioritized daily/upcoming view over a feature-heavy dashboard.

---

## H3 — Fast capture is critical

**Hypothesis:** Users will not maintain the product if adding information is slower or more annoying than Notes/Reminders.

**Evidence to seek:**
- capture method currently used;
- acceptable number of fields;
- whether natural language is preferred;
- willingness to correct AI suggestions.

**Pass signal:** users strongly prefer a one-step or near-one-step capture flow.

---

## H4 — The Things model is intuitive

**Hypothesis:** Users naturally understand personal admin through real-world objects/entities such as car, home, device, pet or service.

**Evidence to seek:**
- how people currently find documents;
- whether documents, costs and reminders are mentally connected to the same object;
- whether an object-first detail page reduces search effort.

**Pass signal:** most participants naturally describe admin tasks in object context.

---

## H5 — Recurring admin is valuable enough to return

**Hypothesis:** Recurring reminders for bills, renewals and maintenance create repeat usage.

**Evidence to seek:**
- recurring tasks today;
- missed recurring obligations;
- time between repeats;
- current reminder methods.

**Pass signal:** repeated high-frequency recurring workflows with weak current handling.

---

## H6 — Notifications need consolidation

**Hypothesis:** Users prefer a small number of high-signal notifications instead of many individual notifications.

**Evidence to seek:**
- notifications currently disabled;
- snooze behavior;
- notification timing problems;
- number of ignored reminders.

**Pass signal:** users describe notification volume/timing as a significant problem.

---

## H7 — Payments belong in life admin, but not as banking

**Hypothesis:** Users need subscription/bill visibility, but do not want this product to replace their banking app.

**Evidence to seek:**
- where bills/subscriptions are currently tracked;
- what they need to remember versus merely observe;
- whether they manually track renewal dates.

**Pass signal:** clear demand for recurring payment visibility without a need for full banking features.

---

## H8 — Documents are useful when contextual

**Hypothesis:** The value of document storage comes primarily from finding the right document at the right time.

**Evidence to seek:**
- document retrieval stories;
- expired/missing document problems;
- whether users care about document folders versus object context;
- whether reminders connected to documents are useful.

**Pass signal:** repeated "I needed X document" stories with poor retrieval workflows.

---

## H9 — Household sharing is a real extension

**Hypothesis:** Couples/families want shared admin while retaining private personal data.

**Evidence to seek:**
- shared bills;
- shared vehicles/home;
- assigned responsibilities;
- current shared tools.

**Pass signal:** repeated current use of shared notes/calendars/chats for household admin and frustration with them.

---

## H10 — AI should remove entry work, not become the product

**Hypothesis:** Users will trust AI more when it extracts structured data and asks for confirmation than when it autonomously makes changes.

**Evidence to seek:**
- willingness to upload documents;
- tolerance for correction;
- trust boundaries;
- preferred confirmation patterns.

**Pass signal:** users value extraction/organization but insist on review for important dates and financial data.
